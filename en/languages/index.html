How it works
01

Set the commitment

Goal, minutes per day, days of the week, start date and preferred times. Subjects, topics, materials and pages stay organized in one place.

02

Log the session

A timer for those who study with the app open; quick manual logging for those who studied and remembered later. Works offline and syncs when you are back.

03

Catch up when you fall behind

A day with nothing logged becomes time to make up, by the rule you chose. Make it all up today or spread it over the next days — the plan compares before and after.

Consistency, not fluencyThe report shows days with logs and time per front. It doesn’t assess pronunciation, vocabulary or level.
A tone you chooseWarm, direct or firm. The daily message changes; the nudge never turns into guilt.
Unlogged day ≠ lost dayForgot to log it? Log it later. Didn’t study? It becomes time to make up, by the rule you chose.
Planned breakA trip, a college exam, a heavy week at work: mark the break and owed time doesn’t grow.

FAQ

Questions from language learners

Which languages can I track?

More than 90. English is preselected when you create the goal, and you can switch to Spanish, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Libras, ASL and many more. If yours isn’t listed, pick “Other language” and name it whatever you like.

Can I study two languages at the same time?

Yes. Each language is a goal, with its own target, days and balance. Free has 1 active goal; Essential, up to 3; Complete, no limit.

Does the app teach the language?

No. It has no lessons, exercises or grading. It organizes the studying you already do — course, podcast, series, book — and tracks the time spent on each front.

Does it work with any course or method?

Yes. In-person or online course, private lessons, a language app, a textbook, an exchange program: the goal is yours, the material is yours. The app logs the time and keeps the plan.

Can I split time by front — listening, vocabulary, grammar, speaking?

Yes. Each front is a topic inside the goal. When logging a session, you pick the topic; the report shows how much time went to each.

Does listening to a podcast on the commute count?

It does, if you want it to. Manually log the duration and topic when you remember; the day’s balance adjusts.

What if 60 minutes a day doesn’t fit my week?

Set the goal that fits — 20, 30, 45 minutes — and the days of the week. Hard weeks and holidays go in as planned breaks, without becoming debt.

Does the report measure fluency?

No. It measures consistency and time per goal and per front. Fluency shows in your conversations, tests and in what you understand of what you hear and read.
